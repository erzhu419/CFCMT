\begin{abstract}
Traffic-signal transfer requires reliable action ranking as topology, demand
and signal programs change across networks.  We introduce anchored
Causal-Factored Cross-City Model Transfer (CFCMT), which predicts normalized
cost differences between feasible phases from matched simulator states.
A parent-restricted rigid anchor and an antisymmetric all-pair model are
blended through seed-blocked target validation before deployment.
Target counterfactual labels make this an offline-adaptation method.
Development on 18 Simulation of Urban MObility (SUMO) networks from seven city
groups reduced leave-one-city-out normalized action regret by 9.84\% on a held-out seed;
six of seven groups improved.  On two external networks, 56 matched 3,600-s
rollouts showed a 6.27\% reduction in all-departed waiting relative to a
same-information dense residual baseline.  A separate preregistered 64-seed
comparison found a 3.04\% Jinan gain but a 7.87\% Los Angeles regression,
rejecting joint two-city benefit over a legacy near-target-only controller.
A subsequent target-offline selector returned target-only control for Los
Angeles and admitted a Hangzhou source component for Jinan.
On 56 further Jinan seeds, the admitted controller reduced waiting by 4.53\%
relative to a lower-capacity zero-source-row target model.
The paired 95\% interval was 4.28--4.79\%, and all 56 seeds improved.
Pressure policies remained stronger.  These results support structured
action-contrast modelling over dense residual transfer and a reproducible
Jinan controller-pair gain, while leaving source-row attribution and broader
cross-city benefit unresolved.
\end{abstract}
