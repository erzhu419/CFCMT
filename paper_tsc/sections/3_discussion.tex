\section{Discussion}

The central result concerns the alignment between model structure and the
control decision.  CFCMT estimates normalized action contrasts, retains a
rigid local anchor and selects an all-pair correction on complete held-out
target seeds.  This design reduced external offline regret and closed-loop
waiting relative to a same-information dense residual.  The dense comparator
also receives matched action contrasts, so reference subtraction alone cannot
explain the gain.  The comparison supports the combined structural design
rather than identifying each restriction's individual contribution.

The frozen-model confirmation strengthens this controller comparison beyond
the original two-seed matrix.  Ten new paired simulator seeds reproduced the
dense-baseline advantage without refitting or tuning: waiting decreased by
4.93\%, with a paired 95\% interval of 2.64--7.31\%.  Completion increased
while system burden decreased.  The ten-seed result shows that the original
6.27\% estimate was not confined to its two evaluation seeds, while the
Los Angeles regressions on four new seeds expose remaining local variability
(\Cref{sec:v43-dense-fresh10}).

This result clarifies why the earlier full mechanism-world-model route was
not retained as the final controller.  MC-WM decomposed queue propagation,
red accumulation, mobility, spillback and control cost.  These components
support diagnosis, but their prediction errors accumulate before the model
answers the narrower phase-selection question.  Good next-state or mechanism
loss does not guarantee correct action ordering.  Under limited target
support, flexible latent or graph paths can also dominate the rigid signal.
The final design preserves declared parents and local mechanism features,
then adds an antisymmetric ranking correction with a measurable, bounded
effect.

The controller comparisons show that transferred information has
network-dependent value.  V91 held target labels, feature semantics, model
family and execution fixed.  Across 64 fresh seeds, CFCMT reduced Jinan
waiting by 3.045\%, with all 64 seed-level city contrasts favourable.
Los Angeles waiting increased by 7.865\%, with only 22 of 64 contrasts
favourable.  The equal-city mean rejected a joint two-city source-benefit
claim.  These opposite effects show why source transfer needs an explicit
abstention option rather than an assumption of benefit.

V98 tested that option through a separate post-V91 target-offline selector.
Before its fresh rollouts, the selector returned exact target-only control
for Los Angeles and admitted a Hangzhou source component at mass one for
Jinan.  On 56 further Jinan seeds (336 rollouts), the admitted controller
reduced waiting by 4.527\% against a strict zero-source-row target model.
The paired 95\% interval was 4.279--4.787\%, and all 56 contrasts were
favourable (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank probability value
$7.5475\times10^{-11}$).  The decisions preceded the rollouts, so the gain
reflects the preselected Jinan controller comparison
(\Cref{sec:supp-v98-confirmation}).

Pressure control remains the stronger operational reference on both external
cities.  MaxPressure and PhasePressure achieved lower system vehicle-hours
and slightly higher completion than CFCMT.  Queue pressure directly reflects
service imbalance, giving these rules a strong local control signal.
CFCMT's advantage over a dense residual supports structured transfer, while
the pressure comparison identifies where the learned controller remains
weaker.  The discarded pressure-guard experiments showed a similar boundary
(\Cref{sec:supp-discarded-guard,tab:discarded-guard-600,tab:discarded-guard-3600}).
Those pressure guards are absent from the final V43 policy.

\subsection{Limitations}

The formal external evidence contains two independent cities.  Multiple Jinan
demand scenarios add within-city coverage but do not add independent city
units.  Los Angeles and Jinan are CityFlow-derived networks converted and
semantically audited for SUMO, rather than calibrated city-wide digital
twins.  Training counterfactuals and evaluation share the same SUMO physics
family.  The study therefore evaluates cross-network transfer in simulation,
with field transfer left untested.

Target counterfactual labels also define the information scope.  The
uncalibrated target simulator reduces calibration effort, but its action
outcomes remain labelled target data.  The external comparison uses 165 and
911 adaptation groups and is an offline-adaptation result, not a zero-shot
result.  The seven-policy matrix uses two seeds; the independent frozen-model
dense comparison adds ten new seeds on the same four scenarios.  V91 uses
64 seeds over the same two target geometries, and V98 uses 56 further seeds on
Jinan.
These protocols remain separate, and their intervals describe the stated
networks and demand settings.  These evaluations use a fixed 3,600-s horizon
and include unfinished vehicles.  Longer incidents, non-recurrent demand and
detector failure remain untested.

Source-row attribution has two specific limits.  V91's comparator assigns source-labelled
samples zero weight in deployed scoring, but its retained source prior can
resolve exact prediction ties.  Its comparator is therefore legacy
near-target-only rather than strictly source-free.  V98 uses a strict
zero-source-row target model, but that model has lower capacity than the
selected controller.  Both V98 arms also share a risk multiplier of 0.5
inherited from earlier Jinan closed-loop development.  The comparison
establishes its declared controller-pair gain, without isolating source rows
at fixed architecture or demonstrating superiority over PhasePressure.

The later source diagnostics reinforce this distinction between available
headroom and a reliable selection rule.  Target-seed-blocked gates either
retained city regressions or selected target only throughout the seven
development cities (\Cref{sec:supp-source-gate,tab:source-gate-development}).
Mechanism-specific priors, budget-aware shrinkage, local context and
state-conditioned or hierarchical utility did not resolve the source-identity
controls.  Forced source arms improved five of seven city means for the final
target-calibrated prior, but its city interval crossed zero.  The state-level
utility gate that passed offline development caused a large Cologne
closed-loop regression before matrix expansion.
Development-only V123/V157A contrasts were superseded after a Jinan
domain-handling mismatch was found; V157B corrected B100 on the reused
selector but remained worse than PhasePressure.  V157C retained an invalid
seed in its incomplete native diagnostic, and V158/V159 failed their frozen
native-prefix and endpoint-calibration gates.  Their negative results and
complete quantitative record are retained in
\Cref{sec:supp-source-remedy,tab:source-remedy-ladder}.
These diagnostics leave multicity source-benefit identification unresolved.

The sequential development record also matters.  A diagnostic external seed
exposed a one-city regression, prompting removal of an arbitrary adaptation
cap and stricter complete-seed cross-fitting.  That seed was permanently
excluded, and a new seed authorized the final test.  The final V43 cache,
selectors, deployment models and closed-loop matrix were frozen before their
respective next stages.  This sequence is not equivalent to a single
untouched preregistration (\Cref{sec:supp-development-history,sec:supp-artifact-chain}).
A separate execution-layer programme's favourable 54-seed adaptive estimate
shrank almost to zero on 64 newly generated Jinan seeds.
It failed its preregistered bootstrap, sign-test and worst-seed criteria
(\Cref{sec:supp-postfreeze-stress,tab:postfreeze-stress}).
Its different 120-s latent originator and global cooldown distinguish it
from the V43 comparison.

Same-protocol CrossLight, X-Light, MetaLight and GESA reproductions remain a
substantive comparison gap.  Their published experiments differ in target
interaction budgets, state and action interfaces, simulators and training
objectives.  A broader benchmark should implement these systems behind the
same phase executor and distinguish zero labels, passive transitions and
matched counterfactual actions.  Native-protocol scores cannot provide those
paired comparisons (\Cref{sec:supp-native-rl}).

Future work should freeze the link between source compatibility and action
ranking before evaluation on additional independent cities.
A calibrated digital twin or prospective signal intervention could test
whether the pairwise advantage survives simulator mismatch.
Passive detector or trajectory records could adapt state representations,
but do not label unexecuted counterfactual actions.
Objectives involving multimodal priority, spillback risk, emissions or
network-level coordination may reveal value beyond a pressure score.
The present evidence supports matched action contrasts relative to dense
residual transfer and a separately confirmed Jinan controller-pair gain.
The broader question is when source mechanisms improve control beyond both
a target-only model and a strong local rule.
