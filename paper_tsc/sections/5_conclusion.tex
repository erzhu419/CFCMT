\section{Conclusion}

Anchored CFCMT formulates cross-network signal adaptation as normalized,
matched action ranking.  Its parent-restricted anchor and antisymmetric
correction reduced held-out action regret in development and on two external
networks.  In the frozen external comparison, CFCMT reduced all-departed
waiting by 6.27\% relative to a same-information dense residual baseline.
Ten new paired seeds across the same four scenarios independently confirmed
this advantage with both models frozen: waiting decreased by 4.93\%, with a
paired 95\% interval of 2.64--7.31\% and improvement in nine of ten seed-level
contrasts.

Controller gains depended on the target network.  The 64-seed V91 comparison
improved Jinan waiting by 3.045\% but worsened Los Angeles waiting by 7.865\%
relative to its legacy near-target-only comparator.
A separate target-offline selector subsequently returned target-only control
for Los Angeles and admitted a Hangzhou source component for Jinan.
Across 56 further Jinan seeds, the admitted controller reduced waiting by
4.527\%, with a paired 95\% interval of 4.279--4.787\% and improvement on every
seed.  This V98 result establishes a reproducible controller-pair gain;
its lower-capacity zero-source-row comparator leaves source-row attribution
unresolved.

The evidence supports structured action-contrast modelling and selective
target adaptation within the tested SUMO benchmarks.
MaxPressure and PhasePressure remained stronger controllers, and later
source-selection and native-calibration tests did not establish a general
source benefit.  Broader transfer claims require independent target networks
and evaluation beyond the shared simulator physics.
