\section{Conclusion}

Cross-network traffic-signal transfer improved when the learned object was
reduced from absolute dynamics to normalized, matched action contrasts.
Anchoring a parent-restricted score and correcting it with an antisymmetric
all-action model reduced seed-held-out action regret in development and in two
external city-derived networks.  The same frozen models improved closed-loop
waiting time over a dense residual and produced favourable simulator-only and
rigid-anchor point estimates, while remaining behind MaxPressure and phase
pressure.  Crucially, the 64-seed v91 legacy near-target-only comparison
rejected a general source-contribution claim: CFCMT reduced Jinan waiting by
3.045\% with all 64 seed-level contrasts favourable, but increased Los Angeles
waiting by 7.865\% with only 22 of 64 contrasts favourable.  A separate
post-v91 target-offline selector selected exact target-only control for Los
Angeles and admitted a Hangzhou source component for Jinan before fresh
rollouts.  Across 56 further Jinan seeds (336 rollouts), that admitted
controller reduced waiting by 4.527\% relative to a strict zero-source-row
target model (paired 95\% interval, 4.279--4.787\%), with all 56 contrasts
favourable.  CFCMT therefore supports an auditable action-contrast architecture
and a preselected V98 Jinan controller-pair gain.  The latter comparison does
not isolate source rows at fixed architecture because its strict target
comparator has lower capacity, and both arms share a risk
multiplier of 0.5 inherited from earlier Jinan development.  A subsequent
seven-city remedy ladder confirmed source headroom but found no general deployable
selector that passed source-identity and closed-loop non-degradation checks.
Establishing broader cross-city source-row benefit will require more independent
target cities and either logged interventions, a validated digital twin or
prospective field control; the present evidence also does not establish
superiority over phase pressure.
