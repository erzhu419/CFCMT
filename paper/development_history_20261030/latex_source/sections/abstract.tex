\begin{abstract}
Road networks differ in junction layout, traffic demand, and available signal phases. A transferred controller must compare phase choices under these different conditions. We study a supervised transfer method that learns the cost difference between actions applied to the same simulated traffic state. A predictor restricted to local traffic features provides a reference, while a second predictor compares every pair of feasible phases. Development uses 18 networks in seven groups, followed by evaluation on two external networks. The final controller selects source-group components or a target anchor using target action labels and eight closed-loop development seeds, then freezes its predictors and execution guard. The complete comparison comprises 1,344 one-hour simulations across six controllers, four scenarios, and 56 paired seeds. Mean city-wise relative waiting decreases by 2.42\% against MaxPressure (95\% interval for the reduction, 0.66--4.58\%), with 40 of 56 seed means favorable. Against PhasePressure, the reduction is 1.91\%, although its interval crosses zero. Relative waiting also decreases by 2.30\% against the legacy target anchor and 9.40\% against the dense linear residual model. The selected controller uses an Atlanta component in Jinan and the target anchor in Los Angeles. These results support structured action prediction and the tested source-selection procedure on the two target networks, with all adaptation and selection completed before evaluation.
\end{abstract}
